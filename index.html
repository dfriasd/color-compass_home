Where it started

It started with a trip to Colombia.

Growing up in the United States, and then visiting Colombia — my mother's home country — showed me two very different worlds. At home, crayons and colored pencils were ordinary. In the communities I visited, they were hard to come by.

What if the materials sitting unused here could become useful somewhere else?

As featured in The Independent Florida Alligator, August 2025.

2019

750 crayons, colored pencils & markers collected

With help from family and friends, I started collecting supplies. The first effort came to roughly 750 items.

Getting them to Colombia turned out to be its own problem — international shipping isn't cheap.

The supplies were ready. Shipping them wasn't.

So I sold two pairs of my own shoes — a pair of New Balances and a pair of Air Maxes — to help cover the cost.

That first shipment is where Color Compass actually began.

Building a system

One shipment turned into a question: how do I make this repeatable?

I started reaching past family and friends — emailing schools, calling businesses, walking into local shops in Gainesville and explaining what I was building. I asked for sponsorships to help cover Color Compass's biggest cost: international shipping. Over time, that turned into real relationships with schools, businesses, and community organizations — and a sponsorship model where supporters could fund the logistics while donated materials went straight to the communities that needed them.

How it works

Getting a box of crayons from a Gainesville classroom to a school abroad takes more than good intentions.

Six steps, and each one solves a different version of the same problem: getting the right materials to the right place without wasting what's donated.

Discover need

Understand what a school or community actually needs before anything gets collected.

Collect

Gather new or gently used supplies through schools, families, businesses, and collection events.

Organize

Sort and prepare materials for distribution.

Route

Work out the most efficient way to get materials where they're going — sponsors, travelers, partners, shipping.

Deliver

Partner with people on the ground who put materials directly into kids' hands.

Learn

Evaluate costs, needs, and logistics to make the next round more effective.

"Why don't you just send money?"

An advisor connected to foreign missions asked me that, and it was a good question. International shipping can be expensive — sometimes what it costs to ship a box could buy a lot more locally. Instead of dismissing it, I started comparing the two routes.

Ship donated supplies

  • Shipping cost
  • Quantity available
  • Destination access

Purchase locally

  • Exchange rate
  • Local prices
  • Purchasing power

Impact isn't about moving the most boxes. It's about making the resources available create the most value.

Beyond Colombia — the impact so far

It started with 750. It hasn't stopped.

Color Compass began as an effort focused on Colombia. Over time, the network grew — reaching communities across Latin America and Africa through schools, nonprofits, sponsors, travelers, and people willing to help materials reach where they were needed.

Who's behind it

Daniel Frias-Duran

I founded Color Compass in 2019, not long after a childhood trip to Colombia made the gap in access to creative and educational resources impossible to ignore. I didn't start out understanding nonprofits, sponsorships, international logistics, or resource allocation — I learned those things because the project kept handing me the next problem to solve.
